# LinkedIn Content Engine for Greg

## Brand Thesis
"I build with AI. I'll tell you what's real."
Honest business operator's take on AI. Not an evangelist. Not a doom-sayer.

## Voice
- Conversational, warm, specific
- Smart friend over chai

## Format Rules (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
- One sentence per line
- Blank line between each sentence
- Staircase rhythm (short → long → punchy)
- Ends with a genuine question (not rhetorical)

## Hook Patterns (Priority Order)

### 1. The Contrarian
"Everyone says AI will [common belief]. But here's what actually happens in an Indian business..."
*Best for: Monday (set the week's tone)*

### 2. The Specific Number
"I tested [N] AI tools for [specific Indian business task]. Only [low number] survived."
*Best for: Thursday (utility)*

### 3. The "Nobody Talks About"
"The one thing nobody tells you about deploying AI in [Indian context]..."
*Best for: Wednesday (behavioral insight)*

### 4. The Pattern Interrupt
"My 8yo just [unexpected AI behavior]. It changed how I think about adoption."
*Best for: Friday (personal connection)*

### 5. The Confession
"I was wrong about AI [X]. Here's what changed my mind."
*Best for: When you got something wrong — builds trust*

### 6. The Insider's View
"I work at a VoiceAI company. Here's what I see that the media misses."
*Best for: Establishing authority*

### 7. The Message-Yourself (Internal EA) Hook
"The person I text most is myself. And I'm not embarrassed about it."
*Best for: Tools, productivity, personal workflow hacks — works because it names a behavior everyone does but nobody admits*
*Example (from actual Perisclaw post): "The person I speak to the most on WhatsApp is myself. And I'm loving it 🦞" — curiosity-based, relatable, slightly confessional*

## Hook Strength Spectrum

Not all hooks are equal. When analyzing past posts or drafting new ones, classify the hook on this spectrum:

| Level | Pattern | Example | Why It Misses/Hits |
|-------|---------|---------|-------------------|
| **Level 1 — Good** | Confession/observation with NO tension | "I talk to myself most on WhatsApp." | Curiosity hook, but no reason to engage. Reader scrolls. |
| **Level 2 — Better** | Confession + implication (stakes) | "I checked my WhatsApp stats. My #1 contact is myself. Here's why I'm not embarrassed." | Creates tension, promises resolution. Reader wants the payoff. |
| **Level 3 — Best** | Confession + implication + demand/challenge | "I message myself more than my wife. Before you judge — this one tool fixed the chaos." | Creates tension, makes promise, implicitly dares reader to disagree. Highest engagement. |

**Test for Level 3:** Cover the product/tool name. If the hook still makes you want to click, it's Level 3. If the hook reads as a passive observation without the product, it's Level 1 or 2 — rewrite it to have stakes.

Always aim for Level 3. In the post-mortem diagnostic, include which level the hook hit.

## The Comment Seed Tactic (Engagement Multiplier)

**Don't embed the engagement question in the post body.** Post it as the first comment immediately after publishing:

- Post says: "Here's what Perisclaw caught in my WhatsApp today."
- First comment (30 seconds later): *"Honest question — does anyone else feel like their WhatsApp is getting out of control? Or is it just me?"*

**Why it works:**
- People reply to the COMMENT instead of the post — both count as engagement
- Bypasses the "please like/comment" awkwardness
- The question should be answerable in **3 words** — easy answers feed comments, comments feed the algorithm

**Place in cadence:** Drop the seed comment immediately after publishing, before walking away. Reply to every single reply within 30 minutes to keep the thread alive.

## Why Document Posts Outperform Images

LinkedIn's algorithm heavily favors document posts (PDF uploaded as swipeable carousel) over static images. Here's the mechanic:

| Format | Avg Time on Content | Engagement Signals Generated |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------------|
| Text/Image | ~3-6 seconds | 1 (pause, maybe scroll) |
| Video | ~15-60 seconds | 1 (play event) |
| **Document/Carousel** | **~20-90 seconds** | **N (one per swipe)** |

**Root cause (platform incentives):** LinkedIn optimizes for time-on-platform per session. A 5-page document forces *active* engagement — each swipe is a decision point and generates an interaction signal. The algorithm reads high swipe-through rates as "quality content" and boosts it.

**Follow-up specific for Perisclaw content:** Next post should be a 5-slide carousel:
1. Title card: bold white text on dark background
2. The problem: screenshot of chaotic group chat with red circles
3. The moment: Perisclaw surfacing a missed @mention
4. The result: commitment tracked + reminder set
5. CTA: link to try it

**General principle:** Whenever the post format choice is yours, default to document/carousel over single image. The format itself gives you a 2-3x reach multiplier before accounting for content quality.

## Weekly Content Cadence (Starting: 1 Post/Week — Monday Deep Dive Only)

Greg is starting with **one high-quality post per week** — the Monday deep dive. No Wed/Fri posts until the Monday rhythm is muscle memory (minimum 4-6 weeks). When ready, we scale up.

**Important:** This is the *starting target*, not a hard lock. Greg decides what to post and when. If he posts on a Friday instead of Monday (e.g., a personal/Pattern Interrupt post), the role is to support that choice, not police the cadence. The Saturday follow-up should account for whether a topic was ever picked (see branching logic below).

| Day | Type | What Happens |
|-----|------|-------------|
| Thu (prior week) | Topic seeds | John suggests 1-2 deep dive topics for Monday |
| Sat (prior week) | Spar reminder | John checks if Greg picked a topic (see branching logic) |
| Sun | Calendar | Weekly calendar with topic suggestions |
| Mon | Deep Dive | Greg leads. We spar. John drafts Medium article. Greg publishes. |

### Deep Dive Monday Workflow
- Greg leads the thought — he decides the topic and angle
- I suggest 1-2 deep dive topics on **Thursday** (as topic seeds) for him to consider before the weekend
- Once Greg picks a topic, we **spar** — I push his thinking, challenge assumptions, ask "why this, why now"
- Based on our conversation, I compose the Medium article draft
- Greg reviews, refines, and publishes on Medium
- The LinkedIn post links to the Medium article

### Saturday Follow-Up Logic (Branching)
The Saturday "spar reminder" has two paths depending on whether Greg selected a topic from Thursday's suggestions:

**Path A — Topic was selected:** Brief check-in. "Greg, you picked [topic] for Monday. Ready to spar? Reply with your angle."

**Path B — Topic was NOT selected (no engagement with Thursday's suggestions, or Greg posted something unrelated on Friday):** Acknowledge the gap, then offer 2 quick topic suggestions tied to current news. Don't write the post yet — just enough to get him to choose. Format: "Greg — no Monday topic picked yet. Here are two angles from this week's news that are timely: [Option 1] / [Option 2]. Pick one and let's spar."

**Path C — Greg explicitly deferred or said he'd skip Monday:** Respect the deferral. Offer to move the topic to next week. No follow-up needed until Sunday's calendar.

**Pitfall:** Don't repeat Thursday's exact suggestions verbatim on Saturday. If Greg ignored them on Thursday, the same language won't land better two days later. Reframe the same core angle through a different lens (e.g., if Thursday suggested "Cloudflare layoffs as a signal for Indian IT," Saturday could frame it as "what services CEOs won't say publicly").

### Accountability Tracking
- I maintain a simple tracker: topic → discussed? → drafted? → published?
- If Greg says "I want to write about X" and doesn't follow up in 3 days, I remind him
- If a deep dive topic goes undiscussed past Thursday, I flag it and suggest moving it to next week

## Capitalization Protocol (When a Post Gets Traction)

When a post performs above baseline (1%+ engagement rate, comments from non-peers, reposts from tagged accounts), follow this protocol to maximize compounding returns:

### Immediate Actions (Within 1 Hour)

1. **Reply to every comment within 30 minutes.** Not "thanks!" — ask a follow-up question that continues the thread. Each reply is another engagement signal; each renotification brings lurkers back.
2. **Engage with every repost.** Go to each reposter's feed, find the reshare, and leave a valuable comment. Their followers see it. Priority order: company account (tagged) > individual with 5K+ followers > anyone.
3. **Drop a comment on your own post adding new info.** Example after the Perisclaw post: "Didn't expect @Perisclaw to reshare this — if you're curious, here's the link to try it: [link]"
   This renotifies everyone who already engaged AND surfaces the connection.

### Follow-Up Strategy (Within 2-3 Days)

4. **Post a follow-up referencing the traction.** Example: "Someone asked in my last post — 'does this actually save time?' Here's the honest answer..." This rides the algorithmic momentum.
5. **DM people who left thoughtful comments.** A brief, personal message builds genuine network relationships worth more than 100 passive impressions.
6. **Tag the company in the follow-up.** If the first post tagged Perisclaw and they reshared, tag them again in the follow-up with a specific use case. Deepens the relationship.

### What Not to Do

- Don't repost the same content. The post is already working — let it run.
- Don't delete the post. LinkedIn penalizes deleted posts.
- Don't spam commenters with sales pitches. Build relationships, not leads.
- Reply to EVERY comment within 2 hours of posting (algorithm boost)
- Ask follow-up questions in replies (deepens threads)
- DM people who leave thoughtful comments (builds network)
- Share 2-3 other people's posts/day with your take (network growth)

## Post-Mortem Protocol (When a Post Flops)

**Definition of flop:** <200 impressions with zero likes/comments within 24 hours, or any post where engagement ratio <0.5% of impressions.

**IMPORTANT: The Cold-Start Window — do NOT declare flop before 4-6 hours.**
LinkedIn shows a new post to a small sample first (typically 150-300 people). If nobody bites, it dies there. If someone does, the algorithm opens the taps. A post at 180 impressions / 0 engagement after 30-60 minutes is still in the cold-start phase — wait 4-6 hours minimum before running the full Post-Mortem.

**Pattern observed (May 2026):** Greg's post hit 180 impressions with 0 engagement early on, then climbed to 2,089 impressions with ~15 reactions, 6 comments, 3 reposts within 4 hours. The cold-start sample was small and quiet; a later viewer broke the seal and the algorithm expanded reach. This is a **"recovered cold start"** — not a flop, not a winner, but a post that tested the right topic with weak initial execution.

### Follower Context (Greg: 5K followers)

Always factor in Greg's follower count when analyzing post performance. The key metrics:

**Reach Rate** = impressions ÷ follower count × 100
- **~95% reach** (4,731 impressions / 5K followers) = algorithm served nearly entire base. Growth beyond this requires external distribution (reposts from larger accounts).
- **<50% reach** = algorithm didn't push the post hard. Usually a hook/engagement problem.
- **>100% reach** = post is reaching beyond followers via reposts and hashtags. This is the growth zone.

**Engagement to Reach Ratio** is more useful than absolute numbers for a 5K account.

### Engagement Rate Benchmarks

| Rate | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| < 0.5% | Below average — flop territory |
| 0.5% - 1% | Average — fine, room to improve |
| 1% - 3% | Good — strong topic, decent execution |
| 3% - 5% | Excellent — topic + execution clicking |
| 5%+ | Viral — rare, don't expect this |

Calculation: (total reactions + comments + reposts) / impressions × 100. Use this in every post-mortem to frame the verdict objectively.

**Important:** For a 5K follower account, a post hitting ~1.2% engagement (e.g., 24 engagements / 2,089 impressions) is slightly above average but still indicates room for improvement in conversion. The bottleneck shifts from *reach* to *hook quality* once the post reaches 42%+ of followers.

**Takeaway:** If reach rate is >80%, the bottleneck is hook quality and conversion, NOT distribution. Don't blame the algorithm — fix the hook.

### Expansion Ratio (Cold-Start Health Metric)

After the post has had 12+ hours to run, calculate:

**Expansion Ratio** = final impressions ÷ cold-start impressions (at ~30 min)

- **>10x** = strong recovery — the algorithm found an audience
- **5-10x** = moderate — solid topic, execution could be sharper
- **2-5x** = weak — the post didn't find its audience
- **<2x** = dead in cold start

**Example (Greg's Perisclaw post, May 2026):**
- Cold-start (30 min): 180 impressions
- Final (16 hrs): 4,731 impressions
- Expansion ratio: 4,731 ÷ 180 = **26.3x**
- Diagnosis: Strong topic (WhatsApp + AI assistant was fundamentally sound), weak hook delayed the first engagement

Do NOT declare flop before calculating expansion ratio. A small cold-start sample does not mean the post failed.

### 3-Signal Diagnosis

When Greg flags a post that underperformed, run this diagnostic:

**Signal 1: Missing visual anchor**
- No image, video, carousel, or screenshot = algorithm deprioritized it
- Signal strength (for Greg): very strong — nearly every flop fires this one
- Fix: Pair every post with a native visual. Screenshots of WhatsApp threads, dashboards, real outputs work best. Screen recordings even better.
- Exception: Only skip visual if Greg has massive follower gravity (he doesn't yet)

**Signal 2: Tool-first, not problem-first**
- If the first sentence names a product/tool before naming the reader's pain, the post is tool-first
- Fix: Open with the pain (relatable, specific, emotional). The tool is revealed as the cure mid-post, not as the headline.
- Test: Cover the first sentence — if it reads like an ad without the product name, rewrite it
**Signal 3: No engagement hook**

- No question, no controversial take, no invitation to react
- The reader has nothing to add, so they scroll past
- Fix: End with a genuine question about the reader's experience. Ask if they relate. Ask for a counter-opinion. Create a gap the reader wants to fill.
- Counterintuitive: The question should be answerable in 3 words. Hard questions (deep analysis) kill comments. Easy questions (do you relate?) feed them.
- **Hook Strength Spectrum — diagnose which level the post hit:**
  - **Level 1 — Good:** Confession/observation with no tension. "I talk to myself most on WhatsApp." → curiosity hook, but no reason to engage.
  - **Level 2 — Better:** Confession + implication. "I checked my WhatsApp stats. My #1 contact is myself. Here's why I'm not embarrassed." → creates tension, promises resolution.
  - **Level 3 — Best:** Confession + implication + demand. "I message myself more than my wife. Before you judge — this one tool fixed the chaos. Let me show you." → creates tension, makes a promise, and implicitly challenges the reader.
- **Test:** If your hook can be read as a passive observation — rewrite it to have stakes.

### Three-Fork Verdict

#### Fork A: Repost (rewritten, same topic)
When: Topic is genuinely good but execution was wrong. All three signals fired. The topic itself had no chance to prove itself because the execution killed it.
- Wait 5-7 days minimum
- Change the hook, add a visual, flip tool-first to problem-first
- Apply ALL three signal fixes before reposting

#### Fork B: New Angle, Same Topic
When: Topic has potential but the hook pattern was wrong for this audience. Try a different hook from the priority list (e.g. "The Contrarian" if the flop used "The Specific Number").
- Wait 3-5 days
- Lead with a different pain point
#### Fork C: Recovered Cold Start

When: Post started with <200 impressions / 0 engagement but later climbed to 1K+ impressions with real engagement (comments, reposts). This is NOT a flop — the topic resonated, the execution was just weak enough to slow momentum.

- Do NOT repost. The post is already working.
- Take the lesson (which signals fired in the first 30 min) and apply them to the NEXT post
- Publish the next post 2-3 days later — the algorithm now knows Greg posts content people engage with
- Use a different topic but apply all signal fixes from this post
- **Run the Capitalization Protocol** if the recovery produced meaningful engagement (comments, reposts from tagged accounts)

**Recovered Cold Start Pattern (observed May 2026):**
Greg's Perisclaw post followed this exact arc:
- 30 min: 180 impressions, 0 engagement → cold-start dip
- 4 hours: 2,089 impressions, ~15 reactions, 6 comments, 3 reposts → 11.6x expansion
- 16 hours: 4,731 impressions → ~95% follower reach
- Key lesson: A small audience sample was silent; one later viewer broke the seal. The post needed a stronger hook to accelerate that first engagement, but the topic (WhatsApp + AI assistant) was fundamentally sound.

**Expansion ratio:** 4,731 ÷ 180 = **26.3x** — strong recovery. Algorithm found the audience once engagement broke the seal.

**Why this happens:** LinkedIn shows a post to ~150-300 people first (the cold-start sample). If nobody engages, it dies there. If ONE person does, the algorithm opens the taps to a wider circle. The Perisclaw post needed ~300 silent views before someone engaged — that's normal, not a failure.

**What to tell Greg:** "Your post didn't flop — it recovered. 26x expansion ratio means the algorithm saw engagement and pushed it. The issue wasn't the topic, it was the hook. First 100 people saw 'I talk to myself' and scrolled. Person #107 read the screenshot and replied. Next post with a stronger hook will see that first engagement happen at person #12 instead of #107."

**Tip:** Calculate the "expansion ratio" (final impressions / cold-start impressions) to quantify the recovery. >5x = the algorithm saw engagement and opened up. <2x = the post died in cold start.

#### Fork D: Let Go
When: The topic itself didn't resonate — even after 24 hours the post sits at <500 impressions with near-zero engagement (and it had a visual, problem-first framing, and a hook). The audience didn't care about the topic.
- Shelve the topic for 4-6 weeks
- Try again with fresh framing later
- Don't delete the original flop. LinkedIn penalizes deleted posts. Let it sit and fade naturally.
