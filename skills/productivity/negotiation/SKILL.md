# Negotiation (Greg's deals)

Draft negotiation messages and recommend moves for Greg's price/term negotiations. Learned
Oct 2026 (cab-vendor negotiation) at Greg's explicit ask: negotiate for a better price every
time, without sounding desperate, while keeping control and playing game-theory optimal.

## Core stance

- **Never show desperation.** A buyer who sounds eager to close loses the anchor.
- **Project control, not urgency.** "I'd like to close today" is leverage only if paired with
  a credible alternative (BATNA). Name the competitors.
- **Ask don't demand.** Calibrated "what/how" questions keep the other side solving YOUR problem.
- **Always re-anchor lower on a first good quote.** Good price ≠ final price. An initial
  low ask locks the range; conceding is cheap, moving down later is hard.

## Pre-flight checklist (before any message)

1. **BATNA** — Greg's walk-away alternative (the competing vendor + its number). Quote it by name;
   a named competitor is a real threat, a vague "others are cheaper" is noise.
2. **ZOPA** — the plausible band between what Greg will accept and what the vendor will take.
   Ground it in market data (official rate charts, rival listings), not vibes.
3. **Anchor** — the first number on the table anchors the whole negotiation. If Greg makes the
   first offer, open below target (Ackerman: 65%→85%→95%→100% of the target, with calibrated
   questions in between to slow it down).
4. **Ask + walk-away** — put a specific ask ("35K all-in") plus a concrete accept condition
   ("I'll pay the advance now"). A number without a ready-to-close is a wish; with one it's a move.

## Message anatomy (price push)

1. Thank them, restate their number — signals you heard them.
2. Name the competition and the market ("Shillong Travel Taxi and Assam Cabs are at X").
3. The ask — one specific, just-below-target number, "all-in", no hedging.
4. The close condition — "confirm immediately if you can do X and I'll pay the advance now."
5. One calibrated question for the loose ends (driver food allowance in/out; advance % and method).

## Tactical moves (Chris Voss + game theory)

- **Anchoring** (Tversky-Kahneman): first number shapes the final. Buyer going first → open
  aggressive but not absurd; counter-anchoring is weaker than the first anchor.
- **Ackerman bargaining** (65-85-95-100%): extreme anchor then step up in shrinking increments,
  pauses between. Read as restrained, not desperate.
- **Calibrated questions**: "How am I supposed to do that?", "What would it take to get to X?" —
  never "why" (accusatory). They give the counterpart illusion of control while steering to your goal.
- **Mirror + label**: repeat their last 2-3 words or label their position ("it sounds like 37K is
  already tight") to build rapport and pull more info; follow with silence.
- **Accusation audit**: pre-empt the negatives ("you'll probably tell me that's below your cost").
  Naming it first defuses it.
- **Declining BATNA via competition**: the more credible competitor prices you can cite, the lower
  the vendor's perceived BATNA and the better your split (Nash bargaining).
- **Time value**: sellers discount future uncertainty — a fast, decisive "I close now" offer
  extracts a better price than a drawn-out hedge. Pair urgency WITH a visible alternative.
- **Impasse risk**: over-aggressive first offers kill the deal. Keep the ask inside a plausible
  ZOPA (market rate minus ~5-10%), not a fantasy number.

## Rules

- Draft the message, always run it past Greg before sending (external comms = his confirmation).
- Never invent a competitor price that wasn't actually quoted — a fake anchor that gets checked
  destroys trust and kills the deal.
- Numbers in Greg's currency (₹) with "all-in" spelled out; no markup-style table noise in chat.
- One ask per round; don't dump multiple counter-offers in a single message.
